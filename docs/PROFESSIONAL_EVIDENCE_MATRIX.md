## 3. Status and traceability contract

Each artifact received needs its own record even when several fit one collection slot. Preserve distinct records for each flowchart, timeline, report, and revision. Record:

- Stable artifact reference, collection slot, claim IDs, case/intake label, source role, relevant dates/revision, and current-state/proposed-state designation.
- Provenance and availability: owner-attested existence, received, inspected, or unavailable. Keep these distinct from the editorial verification workflow.
- Exact excerpt/location, what it supports, what it cannot support, personal contribution, inherited/team contribution, and any unresolved authorship questions.
- Verification status using the existing workflow; reviewer identity/role, review date, reasoning, and limits. These fields are currently unassigned, not implicitly approved by the owner statement.
- Stakeholder acceptance, implementation environment/release status, and outcome verification as separate fields, with evidence references or “not confirmed.”
- Permission status, disclosure authority, exact allowed material/version/audience, public-name decision, conditions, and privacy review. Unknown stays Unknown; withheld evidence does not become verified by assertion.
- English/Vietnamese content revisions and language-review status; neither is Approved or Published today.

Traceability target: **owner claim → specific artifact/revision and contribution → review decision → permitted narrative**. Within an analytical example, link **need → goal → requirement → rule where applicable → acceptance criterion → validation evidence/status**. Do not create fictional requirements or IDs for nonexistent artifacts merely to complete that chain. Existing COMP identifiers can be attached only where the sample demonstrates the relevant analytical work.

When available evidence verifies only a discussion or analysis artifact, the narrative must stop at that contribution. Formal acceptance, delivery, and results are additional claims. An authorized private review can support a narrowly stated verification decision without publishing the underlying file; do not imply such a review has happened here.

## 5. Privacy, confidentiality, and unavailable evidence

Do not collect raw confidential meeting notes, recordings, client communications, patient records, employee identifiers, leave/health details, compensation, contract particulars, debt/payment records, tokens, endpoints, or production payloads. Do not copy the CV or its personal contact details into the repository. Public repository access does not confer client permission; a GitHub link is neither authorship verification nor release authorization.

Use neutral artifact metadata and cleared, minimal excerpts. Check diagrams, free text, filenames, comments, hidden layers, export metadata, and indirect identifiers. Removing names is not sufficient. Redaction does not itself grant disclosure rights. Named systems and vendor relationships also need a publication decision; the local drafts retain only the names explicitly supplied for this intake.

If evidence cannot be shared, record “unavailable for review” and narrow the claim to owner attestation. If later authorized, a newly created synthetic example may demonstrate reasoning, with its creation date and simulated nature explicit. It cannot substitute for an original professional deliverable, acceptance record, or production result. No synthetic business rules or example payloads are created in this phase.
