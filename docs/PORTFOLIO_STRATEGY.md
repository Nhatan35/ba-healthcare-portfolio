## 5. Differentiation and credibility

1. Lead with an inspectable reasoning chain: evidence → problem → options → decision → requirements → validation. Each arrow should be explainable, not decorative.
2. Connect one requirement to its business rule, exception case, and acceptance criterion; show why it matters operationally.
3. Make personal contribution visible beside each artifact, with source/date and disclosure limits where available.
4. Treat uncertainty as useful information: identify what is proposed, what was tested, and what remains unknown.
5. Maintain the same claims in both languages while adapting vocabulary and sentence structure naturally.
6. Use the strongest authorized evidence first. A well-executed, clearly labeled simulation can demonstrate analysis; it cannot demonstrate employment or production impact.

Trust signals to collect: sanitized authored artifacts, traceability, dated decisions, verified CV facts, permitted feedback with attribution and consent, and measurements with method and limitations. | Topic | Proposed scope / required confirmation |

| Business problem | Investigate whether fragmented requests, schedule changes, or capacity conflicts cause rework or uncertain appointment status. Do not claim this happens without discovery evidence. |
| Business objectives | Clear booking status, manageable scheduling exceptions, consistent handoffs. Candidate measures: conflict frequency and resolution time; baseline, target, definitions, and observed results all `[USER INPUT REQUIRED]`. |
| Stakeholders | Potential patients/authorized representatives, reception/scheduling staff, clinicians or resource owners, operations, IT, and privacy/security roles; confirm actual participants and decision rights. |

| Evidence/artifacts required | Sanitized workflow observations or synthetic scenario brief, stakeholder map, appointment state model, As-Is/To-Be, rule table, story map, traceability sample, permitted validation notes. All actual evidence `[USER INPUT REQUIRED]`. |
| As-Is / To-Be opportunity | Compare request → capacity check → confirmation → change/cancel handoffs with a proposed explicit status and exception workflow. Do not assume the proposed workflow was deployed. |

| Edge cases | Concurrent requests, clinician absence, duplicate submission, interrupted confirmation, revoked proxy access, time-zone/date change, notification failure after a successful booking. |

## 8. Privacy, contribution, and credible outcomes

These are portfolio publishing rules, not regulatory compliance advice or certification.

- Never request or publish raw patient records, identifiable patient data, real message histories, credentials, confidential client/employer documents, or employee private details. Check text, diagrams, image layers, filenames, metadata, comments, revision history, and download contents.
- Prefer original synthetic examples with no derivation from an identifiable person. Label them **Synthetic example / Ví dụ dùng dữ liệu giả lập**. They may illustrate a method but do not prove a real workflow occurred.
- For real work, first confirm disclosure authority and permitted scope. Redact or recreate only permitted content, remove indirect identifiers, inspect exports, and obtain the appropriate review. A blur layer or replacing names is insufficient evidence of anonymization.
- Treat pseudonymized or allegedly anonymized material as restricted until reidentification risk and publication authority have been reviewed. Where uncertain, use synthetic material and describe limitations. Do not put restricted evidence in the repository or send it to external AI/translation tools.
- Use **Real project / Dự án thực tế** only with confirmed participation and permitted description; **Simulated case study / Nghiên cứu tình huống mô phỏng** for explicitly simulated work. Unknown provenance stays draft, never silently becomes either category.
- Label **Assumption / Giả định**, **Proposed solution / Giải pháp đề xuất**, **Expected benefit / Lợi ích kỳ vọng**, and **Measured outcome / Kết quả đã đo lường** consistently. Do not translate a proposal into a completed achievement.
- A reported metric needs its definition, baseline/comparator, source, period, calculation, author contribution, and limitations. No source means no verified result. Use **Not measured / Chưa đo lường** where accurate; zero is not a substitute for unknown.
- State “I analyzed/documented/proposed” only for verified personal work; separate team implementation and organizational outcomes. Do not claim causal ownership of team results without support.
- Claims about regulatory compliance, clinical safety, certification, or standards implementation require applicable context and authoritative evidence. No such claims are authorized by this strategy.
