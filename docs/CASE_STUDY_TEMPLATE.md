# Healthcare BA Case Study Template

This is an authoring template, not a completed project or evidence of experience. Replace prompts only with supported content. Keep all 19 sections; use `Not available`, `Proposed`, or `Not applicable` with a reason when appropriate. Do not invent material to fill a section.

## Case identity and evidence register

- Title and candidate topic:
- Provenance: real project or simulated case. If unknown, resolve or label unverified before presenting as experience.
- Publication status: draft or reviewed for publication.
- Author's role and contribution; separate team responsibilities:
- Timeframe, organization description, and workflow boundary, only where disclosure is permitted:
- Evidence references: use sanitized IDs, source/date, what each supports, and disclosure limitations.
- Assumptions and open questions: assign IDs and identify how each could be validated.
- Privacy review: confirm synthetic or authorized publishable material, including diagrams, screenshots, and metadata.

Use stable IDs such as `GOAL-01`, `REQ-01`, `BR-01`, `AC-01`, and `EVID-01` when artifacts exist. Trace goals to requirements, rules, acceptance criteria, and validation evidence. Empty template fields do not establish any facts.

## 1. Business Context

Describe the setting, service, workflow boundaries, and constraints. Explain why the process matters to patients, staff, or the organization using permitted evidence.

## 2. Problem Identification

State the observed problem, affected actors, impact, and supporting sources. Separate symptoms from hypotheses and avoid proposing the solution as the problem.

## 3. Business Goals

Define intended value and success criteria. Identify any metric's definition, baseline source, target, and measurement plan. Label proposed targets and unknown baselines explicitly.

## 4. Stakeholder Analysis

Identify roles, needs, influence, decision rights, and conflicting priorities. Distinguish confirmed stakeholders from hypothetical roles; do not imply interviews occurred without evidence.

## 5. Discovery and Clarification

Describe discovery methods actually used, questions asked, evidence collected, ambiguities resolved, and remaining questions. Describe unperformed discovery as a plan.

## 6. As-Is Process

Document the current or explicitly simulated workflow, triggers, actors, handoffs, systems, delays, and outputs. Identify the source and boundary of the model.

## 7. Root Cause Analysis

Connect symptoms to supported causes using an appropriate analysis technique. Describe alternative explanations, evidence gaps, and how causal hypotheses could be tested.

## 8. To-Be Process

Describe the proposed workflow and its relationship to the problem and goals. Explain changed responsibilities, controls, dependencies, and rollout assumptions.

## 9. User Story Mapping

Map user activities to tasks and stories, identify an initial delivery slice, and explain prioritization. Include a role, need, and value for each story; label proposed scope.

## 10. Business Requirements

State the business capabilities or outcomes required, with IDs, rationale, priority, source, and goal links. Keep business needs distinct from implementation choices.

## 11. Functional and Non-functional Requirements

Specify observable functional behavior. Define relevant quality requirements such as availability, accessibility, performance, privacy, and recoverability with testable measures or explicitly unresolved thresholds. Trace both to business needs.

## 12. Business Rules

Record decision rules, conditions, precedence, exceptions, owner/source, and applicability. Label unverified organizational or regulatory rules as assumptions requiring validation.

## 13. BPMN / Process Flow

Provide or describe the appropriate model with actors, events, decisions, handoffs, and exceptions. Use consistent BPMN notation if claiming BPMN; label simpler diagrams as process flows. Include a readable text explanation and artifact status.

## 14. Wireframes

Describe or reference proposed screens and the requirements they address. Explain hierarchy, accessible interaction, key states, and feedback sought. Label concepts separately from shipped interfaces; create actual wireframes only when authorized by the task phase.

## 15. Edge Cases and Exception Flows

Identify relevant conflicts, unavailable resources, duplicate requests, access failures, integration outages, and data inconsistencies. For each applicable case, specify detection, actor/system response, recovery, and escalation without inventing clinical policy.

## 16. Acceptance Criteria

Define testable conditions linked to requirement and rule IDs, using Given/When/Then where useful. Cover normal paths, exceptions, access boundaries, and relevant quality criteria. Distinguish proposed criteria from tests actually passed.

## 17. Solution Trade-offs

Compare viable alternatives, constraints, benefits, costs, risks, and dependencies. Explain the chosen or proposed option, business value, and residual limitations using evidence.

## 18. Validation and Outcomes

Separate validation plans, executed checks, stakeholder feedback, and measured outcomes. For measured results, include source, baseline/comparator, period, method, and limitations. For simulations, report only actual simulated validation and label expected benefits as hypotheses, not production achievements.

## 19. Lessons Learned

Explain what the analysis revealed, what remains uncertain, and what would change next time. Ground reflections in the documented work and identify next validation steps without inventing stakeholder endorsement.
