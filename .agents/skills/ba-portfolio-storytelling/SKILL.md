# Healthcare BA Portfolio Storytelling

Present the analyst's structured problem solving, requirements engineering, process improvement, and systems thinking in clear, professional English. Explain decisions and business value through evidence, not promotional claims.

## Establish provenance and scope

- Read the project brief and case-study template in the repository's `docs` directory when available. Respect the current phase; writing a narrative does not authorize UI, wireframes, publishing, or external communications.
- Identify whether the case is a real project or a simulated case before writing it as experience. Candidate topics are ideas only. If provenance is unknown, label it unverified and seek the missing facts rather than assuming participation.
- Record the actual role, contribution, timeframe, boundaries, and permitted evidence. Separate individual work from team delivery. Never invent achievements, experience, employment, interviews, quotes, approvals, metrics, or outcomes.
- Distinguish facts, assumptions, proposed decisions, targets, and measured results. Use explicit evidence gaps instead of filling them with plausible details. Simulated cases may use clearly labeled synthetic scenarios and assumptions, never fictional professional achievements.

## Required case-study sequence

Use these 19 sections in order for a complete case. For a focused edit, work within the requested sections without needlessly rewriting the rest. Retain missing or inapplicable sections in a full case with an explanation.

1. **Business Context**: Explain the service, operational setting, actors, constraints, and analysis boundary.
2. **Problem Identification**: State the problem, affected parties, impact, and evidence; separate symptoms from hypotheses.
3. **Business Goals**: Define intended value, success criteria, and a measurement approach; distinguish targets from results.
4. **Stakeholder Analysis**: Identify roles, needs, influence, decision rights, and competing priorities; mark hypothetical roles.
5. **Discovery and Clarification**: Explain actual discovery methods, questions, findings, ambiguities, and open issues; label planned discovery.
6. **As-Is Process**: Model the current or simulated workflow, triggers, handoffs, systems, bottlenecks, and evidence sources.
7. **Root Cause Analysis**: Show how evidence supports causes, consider alternative explanations, and identify hypotheses requiring validation.
8. **To-Be Process**: Explain proposed changes, controls, responsibilities, dependencies, and links to the business goals.
9. **User Story Mapping**: Connect activities, tasks, and stories; explain prioritization and proposed delivery slices.
10. **Business Requirements**: Specify business needs with identifiers, rationale, source, priority, and goal links.
11. **Functional and Non-functional Requirements**: Describe observable behavior and testable quality needs; mark unknown thresholds explicitly.
12. **Business Rules**: Record conditions, precedence, exceptions, owners/sources, and applicability; distinguish verified rules from assumptions.
13. **BPMN / Process Flow**: Describe actors, events, decisions, and exception paths consistently; label simplified flows accurately and include a text explanation.
14. **Wireframes**: Connect available or proposed concepts to requirements, states, and accessible interactions. Label proposals and respect phase restrictions on creating actual designs.
15. **Edge Cases and Exception Flows**: Explain applicable conflicts, failure detection, recovery, and escalation with responsible actors.
16. **Acceptance Criteria**: Provide testable conditions linked to requirements and rules, including normal and exception paths; do not imply execution.
17. **Solution Trade-offs**: Compare alternatives and explain the decision, business value, costs, risks, constraints, and residual limitations.
18. **Validation and Outcomes**: Separate validation plans, completed checks, feedback, measured results, and expected benefits. Disclose evidence and limitations.
19. **Lessons Learned**: Ground reflections in actual analysis; explain unresolved questions and next steps.

## Evidence and analytical quality

- Show a concise analytical rationale: observation, evidence, interpretation, alternatives, decision, and expected or validated business value. Do not present unsupported certainty as expertise.
- Use sanitized evidence references with source/date and limitations. Maintain traceability from goals through requirements, business rules, acceptance criteria, and validation evidence using stable IDs where useful.
- Support every reported metric with a definition, source, measurement period, baseline or comparator where relevant, and method. If these are unavailable, say the result is unverified or not measured. Do not turn targets or synthetic numbers into achieved improvements.
- For simulations, describe any validation actually performed as simulated validation. State expected operational benefits as hypotheses and propose how they could be tested.
- Expand acronyms on first use and write precise, readable English. Prefer concrete behavior and decision rationale over jargon and exaggerated claims.

## Healthcare accuracy and privacy

- Analyze relevant actors, scheduling/capacity constraints, identity matching, access permissions, handoffs, exception recovery, and audit needs without assuming all apply to every workflow.
- Distinguish administrative requirements from clinical decisions. Verify applicable clinical, regulatory, or interoperability claims against authoritative sources and state context; never assert compliance or clinical safety without evidence.
- Protect confidential healthcare and patient data. Use synthetic examples by default. Do not include patient-identifiable information, protected health information, credentials, or confidential employer/client material in public narratives, commits, logs, screenshots, or external tools.
- For real cases, use only authorized publishable evidence and review free text, screenshots, diagrams, exports, and metadata for direct or indirect identifiers. Removing a name alone does not make material safe to disclose.
- When evidence cannot be disclosed, describe the permitted analytical method and acknowledge the limitation. Do not reconstruct restricted details or request raw patient records.

## Final review

Check that provenance and contribution are explicit, all requested sections are addressed, claims have evidence or honest uncertainty labels, requirements trace to validation, decisions explain business value, and artifacts contain no confidential data. Report remaining gaps without fabricating completion or expanding the authorized phase.
